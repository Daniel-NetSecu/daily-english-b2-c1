Reading

英语短文 · 两天背诵版

When a Delivery Arrives at the Wrong Time

The convenience of home delivery rests on an assumption that is seldom examined: someone will be available precisely when the courier arrives. Once that assumption fails, an apparently straightforward service becomes a negotiation over timing, responsibility, and consent. The difficulty is not necessarily unwillingness to help, but the gap between what one person proposes and what another is authorised to arrange. A neighbour may be nearby without having agreed to accept a parcel; a collection point may exist without being able to release it immediately. Effective communication therefore requires more than politeness. It means distinguishing a tentative suggestion from a confirmed arrangement, and establishing whose agreement is still outstanding.

Anna discovered this distinction when a courier called while she was across town. Unable to return promptly, she asked whether delivery could be deferred until the following morning. “I cannot guarantee a time slot,” he replied, offering to leave the parcel next door instead. “I would rather not presume on my neighbour,” she said. “Could you clarify whether collection from a local shop is an option?” The courier explained that any diversion required authorisation from customer service. Rather than press him for a promise beyond his remit, Anna requested the appropriate contact details. Her aim was not to secure an immediate concession, but to identify an alternative that could actually be implemented.

The agent subsequently arranged collection for the next afternoon, subject to confirmation that the parcel had reached the shop. Anna checked the opening hours, identification requirements, and deadline before accepting. “Am I right in understanding that I should wait for the collection notification, rather than rely on the estimated arrival time?” she asked. The agent confirmed this and emailed the instructions. Had Anna treated the estimate as a guarantee, she might have made a needless journey. Instead, she collected the parcel after receiving the notification. What resolved the inconvenience was neither persistence alone nor elaborate language, but her insistence on making the conditions of the arrangement explicit.

03

Grammar & Patterns

语法与句型

Had + 主语 + 过去分词, 主语 + might/would have + 过去分词

过去反事实条件句的倒装形式：省略 if，将 had 提至主语前。表示过去并未发生的条件及其可能结果。might have 强调“本可能”，不是必然。

Example: Had Anna treated the estimate as a guarantee, she might have made a needless journey.

04

Quick Practice

快速练习

  1. 句式练习改写为倒装条件句：If I had left without checking, I might have wasted a journey.
