英语短文 · 两天背诵版

The Danger of a Quick Conclusion

People often expect a technical report to provide a simple answer: repair the equipment, replace it, or leave it alone. In practice, the evidence is rarely so complete. An inspection records what could be observed under particular conditions, and its conclusions must be understood within those limits. Trouble begins when a brief summary turns a possibility into a certainty, or treats an unexamined area as a safe one. This is why a useful briefing should distinguish confirmed findings from unanswered questions. Its purpose is not to make uncertainty disappear, but to explain what further information is needed before a sound decision can be made.

A recent incident at a mine workshop illustrated this point. Daniel was asked to summarise a report about corrosion around several roof fixings. Having read only the opening paragraph, a colleague suggested replacing every roof sheet. Another argued that the damage was merely cosmetic. Daniel noticed, however, that neither conclusion was supported by the report. “The corrosion appears to be localised,” he told the manager, “but one section was inaccessible, so we cannot rule out further damage.” Instead of recommending an immediate purchase, he asked the inspector to clarify the scope of the findings and whether any temporary restrictions were required while the assessment continued.

The inspector replied that the unexamined section required access from a different position. Following the additional inspection, the responsible engineer recommended a limited repair rather than complete replacement. Daniel then prepared a short update separating the confirmed damage, the agreed work, and the questions that still needed answers. The manager thanked him for preventing an unnecessary order without dismissing a genuine concern. What had first appeared to be a choice between doing everything and doing nothing had become a specific plan based on evidence. Daniel had not solved the engineering problem himself; he had ensured that the people responsible were answering the right questions before committing resources.

03

Grammar & Patterns

语法与句型

Having + 过去分词, 主语 + 谓语…

完成式分词短语表示该动作先于主句动作发生。分词动作与主句动作的执行者必须一致。文中先读开头段落，再提出更换建议。

Example: Having read only the opening paragraph, a colleague suggested replacing every roof sheet.

04

Quick Practice

快速练习

  1. 句式练习用 Having + 过去分词合并：Daniel checked the photographs. Then he contacted the inspector.
